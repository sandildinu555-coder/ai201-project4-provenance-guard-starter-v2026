## What This Does

When a user submits text, the system takes the following path:
1. **Input Validation:** The system checks if the text is missing, empty, or exceeds 10,000 characters. If it violates these rules, it immediately returns a 400 Bad Request error with a clear explanation.
2. **Signal 1 (Detector):** The text passes through a local 550MB GPT-2 model (`detector.py`) which evaluates how predictable the next words are (context/meaning).
3. **Signal 2 (Stylometry):** The text is analyzed for its physical shape (`stylometry.py`), measuring sentence length spread, punctuation density, and vocabulary variety.
4. **Scoring:** The scores from both signals are combined using a weighted average to produce a final 0-1 confidence score (`scoring.py`).
5. **Labeling:** The numerical score is mapped to one of three plain-English labels (human, AI, or unsure).
6. **Logging:** The decision (including `model_score` and `style_score`) is permanently written to an audit log (`audit.jsonl`).
7. **Response:** The user receives a JSON response containing a unique `content_id`, the confidence score, and the plain-English label. If they disagree, they can use the `content_id` to submit an appeal to the `/appeal` endpoint, which flags the entry as `under_review` in the audit log.

---

## Signals and Scoring

### Signal one — the model signal

**What it measures:** The predictability of the text based on next-word probabilities (using a GPT-2 language model).

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** AI models are designed to generate the most statistically likely next word, producing highly predictable text. Humans write unpredictably, using rare idioms or unexpected phrasing.

**What it can't see:** It cannot recognize that simple, plain English written by a human is inherently predictable, leading to a massive blind spot where it falsely flags basic human writing as AI.

### Signal two — the style signal

**What it measures:** The physical shape of the text by calculating the coefficient of variation of sentence lengths, expressive punctuation density, and type-token ratio.

**Why that might differ between human and AI writing:** AI tends to write uniform, medium-length sentences with minimal expressive punctuation and repetitive vocabulary. Humans write "lumpy" text—mixing a two-word sentence with a twenty-word sentence and using dashes, semicolons, or exclamation marks to express emotion.

**What it can't see:** It cannot read the actual meaning of the words. An attacker can easily manipulate this signal by injecting random punctuation or arbitrarily breaking up sentences without changing the core AI-generated meaning.

### The combining rule

**The rule:** We use a weighted average favoring the model signal slightly more because it reads actual context, while relying heavily on the style signal to rescue simple human writing from being falsely flagged.

**The numbers:** `(0.60 * model_score) + (0.40 * style_score)`

**Where it lives:** `scoring.py::combine_signals`

**A case where my two signals split, and what my rule does with it:** 
If a human writes a very simple, direct email, the detector might flag it as highly predictable AI (`0.80`), but the stylometry will recognize the human sentence variance and punctuation (`0.10`). The 40% style weight pulls the final score down to `0.52`, safely landing it in the "unsure" category rather than falsely accusing the human writer of using AI.

## Label Variants

| Label | Score range | The exact text a reader sees |
|---|---|---|
| high-confidence human | 0.00 – 0.35 | We are highly confident that this text was written by a human. |
| unsure | 0.35 – 0.65 | Our system cannot reliably determine whether this piece was written by a person or generated by AI. |
| high-confidence AI | 0.65 – 1.00 | We are highly confident that this text was generated by AI. |

**Why I worded the "unsure" one this way:**
It is critical to admit the system's limitations directly without sounding accusatory. If a human writer receives this label on their original work, an ambiguous or suspicious message would feel insulting. This wording neutrally states that the tool simply lacks enough evidence, protecting the writer's integrity.

## Rate Limiting

**My limits:** 10 per minute, 200 per day

**Why those numbers and not others:**
A genuine human writer checking drafts or revisions submits a piece every few minutes at most, so 10 requests per minute and 200 per day easily accommodates real interactive writing sessions and even heavy classroom editing without friction. Conversely, an automated red-team script attempting to probe and map our decision thresholds (`0.35` and `0.65`) or brute-force evasion variations needs hundreds or thousands of rapid queries; capping at 10/minute throttles automated threshold-mapping scripts within seconds while protecting the CPU-bound local GPT-2 model from queue exhaustion.

**What a caller counts as:**
Per `creator_id` when a valid non-empty string `creator_id` is provided in the JSON payload, falling back to the caller's IP address (`get_remote_address()`) when `creator_id` is missing, blank, or when the payload is malformed. This prevents a single household or campus NAT sharing one IP from blocking each other as long as they use distinct creator IDs, though a hostile script rotating fake `creator_id` values can bypass per-creator limits while a malformed flood trips the IP limit.

**Diagnoses**

* **Missed Criterion 3 (Label Coverage) & Criterion 2 (Score Spread):**
  * **Stage:** Signal two (`stylometry.py`) and The combining rule (`scoring.py::combine_signals`).
  * **Mechanism:** The system failed to reach the AI label (Threshold: `0.65`) for clearly AI-generated text and compressed the score spread between human (`0.2128`) and AI (`0.5823`) to `0.3695` (below the `0.45` target). While the model signal scored AI text high (`0.6189`), the style signal scored it moderately (`0.5274`). Because the style signal carries a `0.40` weight, it dragged the combined score down to `0.5823`, landing it in the `unsure` band.

* **Broken Evasion Attacks (`EV01`–`EV09` slipping through as Human/Unsure):**
  * **Stage:** Signal two (`stylometry.py`) and Signal one (`detector.py`).
  * **Mechanism:** The evasion inputs added typos, extra punctuation (`EV07`), human opening paragraphs (`EV09`), or kept text below the token window (`EV08`). Our stylometry signal measures sentence length variation and punctuation density. These perturbations artificially raised vocabulary variety and punctuation counts, causing `style_signal` to score them lower (`0.44`–`0.49`) and dragging the combined score below `0.65`.

* **Broken False Positives (`FP01`–`FP08` human text flagged as AI/Unsure) & Prompt Injections (`PI01`–`PI06`):**
  * **Stage:** Signal one (`detector.py`) and Signal two (`stylometry.py`).
  * **Mechanism:** The local GPT-2 model measures token predictability. Plain, formal, rubric-following, or technical human prose (`FP01`–`FP07`) is highly predictable (`model_score` `0.61`–`0.88`) and uniform in sentence length (`style_score` `0.54`–`0.76`), pushing combined scores above `0.65` and falsely accusing human writers. Conversely, prompt injection strings (`PI01`–`PI06`) use unusual imperative formatting that lowers GPT-2 predictability (`0.20`–`0.64`), dropping scores into `unsure` or `human`.

* **Broken Malformed Inputs (`MF02` Server Crash 500 & `MF03`–`MF07`, `RQ02`, `RQ06` returning 200s):**
  * **Stage:** The Route (`app.py::submit`) and Signal one (`detector.py::model_signal`).
  * **Mechanism:** `MF02` submitted a single-character string (`len == 1`), which passed `app.py`'s `not text.strip()` check but triggered an uncaught `ValueError: Text is too short to score — a few sentences at least` inside `detector.py`, crashing the request with a `500`. **What I changed:**
I narrowed the `unsure` band by changing `HUMAN_THRESHOLD` from `0.35` to `0.40` and `AI_THRESHOLD` from `0.65` to `0.55` in `config.py`.

**Which diagnosis pointed at it:**
The Label Coverage and Evasion diagnosis, which showed that clearly AI-generated text (`0.5823`) and lightly perturbed AI text (`EV07` at `0.5508`, `EV09` at `0.5994`) were clustering between `0.55` and `0.64`—stuck in the `unsure` band because `AI_THRESHOLD = 0.65` was too high given the `0.40` weight on `style_score`.

## What's Still Broken

* **Single-Character Crash (`MF02` returning 500) & Non-Prose Inputs (`MF03`–`MF07`, `RQ06` returning 200):** In `app.py::submit`, I would enforce a minimum character/word length (e.g., at least 20 characters and 5 alphabetic words) and catch `ValueError` from `detector.model_signal` so short strings return a `400` instead of a `500`, reject non-string `creator_id` payloads (`RQ06`), and require a minimum ratio of alphabetic characters to reject control-character, emoji-only, and punctuation-only floods (`MF03`–`MF06`).
* **Evasion via Perturbations (`EV01`–`EV06`, `EV08`) & Formal Human False Positives (`FP01`–`FP07`):** I would normalize whitespace/punctuation before running `stylometry.py` and re-calibrate `stylometry.py` so that formal, structured human essays (`FP02`–`FP07`) are not penalized for consistent sentence lengths, which would also widen the score spread (`Criterion 2`).
* **Why I stopped:** Unit 8 restricts changes to the rate limiter, audit log, and a single targeted improvement so the before-and-after runs isolate one variable; modifying `app.py` validation and `stylometry.py` at the same time would make it impossible to attribute which change moved the numbers.

**The trade I'd make if this ran for real:**
If this service ran in production, I would push `AI_THRESHOLD` back up to `0.65` (or `0.70`) and widen the `unsure` band so borderline cases default to `unsure` rather than `ai`. That setting lets more AI-generated text slip through—meaning the platform operator pays a small moderation cost—rather than falsely accusing honest human writers (especially non-native speakers in `FP03` or plain writers in `FP01`), who would otherwise pay with their reputation and trust.
